It is not a bound on the patterned device's scattering error, and it is not subtracted from the measured complex scattering difference. ## QTCAD workflow comparison

The public QTCAD 2.2.6 documentation was checked on 2026-11-13. Its [driven Maxwell transmission-line tutorial](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/tutorials/device/maxwell_driven_tx_line/) demonstrates adaptive meshing with an S-parameter convergence criterion, an example tolerance of 0.001, and an analytical comparison. This is a useful model for checking the requested observable through successive meshes. It solves an electromagnetic transmission line; it does not establish an elastic-wave result for this perforated Ge beam.

The [feature overview](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/introduction/) and [API structure](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/getting_started/API_structure/) describe electronic, electrostatic and electromagnetic capabilities, but do not document the three-dimensional elastic phononic scattering solver required here. Its strain-aware electronic solver and carrier transport are different physical problems. This statement concerns the documented public scope, not undisclosed vendor capabilities. The [official FAQ](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/faq/general/#does-qtcad-support-gpu-acceleration) states that GPU acceleration is not currently supported. Faster hardware alone would not remove spatial discretization error.

If a future device includes a defined semiconductor hole dot, QTCAD can evaluate how a prescribed strain field changes its electronic energies and heavy-hole/light-hole character. `Device.set_strain` accepts a position-dependent tensor, and the Bir–Pikus Hamiltonian uses material deformation potentials. A one-way comparison could interpolate a verified elastic strain field onto the dot mesh and compare the electronic solution with zero strain. That comparison requires the actual heterostructure, gate potentials, deformation potentials, strain amplitude and coordinate convention. It would quantify electronic sensitivity to the supplied field. [QTCAD strain input](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/API_reference/qtcad.device.device/#qtcad.device.device.Device.set_strain) · [Strained-hole tutorial](https://docs.nanoacademic.com/qtcad/tutorials/device/holes_strain/)

The layered mesh extension follows the established idea of sweeping a planar mesh through prescribed thickness layers ([Gmsh structured grids](https://gmsh.info/doc/texinfo/gmsh.html#Structured-grids)). It retains all three displacement components and physical thickness. Here, each triangular prism is split conformingly into affine tetrahedra for the existing P2 solver. It is not a plate reduction, a high-order spectral-element implementation, or an automatic convergence guarantee. Thickness refinement changes the end-face discretization as well as the body; its SAFE reference must be tracked separately.

## Separating port and device resolution

H. Gravenkamp, B. Plestenjak and D. A. Kiefer, *Notes on osculations and mode tracing in semi-analytical waveguide modeling*, Ultrasonics 135, 107112 (2023), [accepted manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06086v2), describes the cross-section discretization and mode-tracing problems of SAFE waveguides. Crossings and avoided crossings motivate field-overlap matching instead of assigning physical identity from sorted eigenvalues alone. Our isolated four-channel study uses overlap matching; it does not implement that paper's general branch-tracing method. The bounded frequency-map pilot links adjacent signed propagating fields with overlap-qualified permutations and cumulative modal gauges. It breaks tracks at failed points, changed channel counts or ambiguous/degenerate modes; this remains sampled correspondence rather than a proof of complete continuous branches between frequencies.

W. Duan and R. Kirby, *A numerical model for the scattering of elastic waves from a non-axisymmetric defect in a pipe*, Finite Elements in Analysis and Design 100, 28-40 (2015), [author manuscript](https://v-lib-bura1.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/11171/1/Fulltext.pdf), separates uniform-guide modal resolution from the local three-dimensional defect mesh and tests their convergence independently. Its pipe geometry and power checks do not supply an error bound or complex-phase threshold for this Ge nanobeam.

The practical choice here is to measure width and thickness sensitivity in the port cross-section first, then compare existing uniform runs to a common finer SAFE phase reference at the original physical end planes. This is our numerical diagnostic strategy, not a claim that either paper validates the present device. A native coarse-port phase discrepancy can grow when error cancellation changes; it cannot by itself rank continuum accuracy. No uniform-guide phase is subtracted from the patterned-device scattering matrix.

A.-S. Bonnet-Ben Dhia, C. Chambeyron and G. Legendre, *On the use of perfectly matched layers in the presence of long or backward propagating guided elastic waves*, Wave Motion 51, 266–283 (2014), derives a modal radiation treatment and tests why ordinary perfectly matched layers can fail for backward modes in isotropic elastic guides. This motivates identifying outgoing modes by energy transport and checking lead extensions at common observation planes before using a finite absorbing termination as an independent benchmark. [Author-hosted article](https://www.ceremade.dauphine.fr/~legendre/papers/Bonnet2014.pdf)

R. N. Patel et al., *Single-Mode Phononic Wire*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 040501 (2018), uses a geometry sweep and optical readout to identify a single-channel band in silicon. Regular longitudinal standing-wave spacing in that band agrees with the independently estimated group velocity. Multichannel spectra in the same study show mixing and irregular spacing. In this project, modal participation and lead-length sensitivity are therefore useful checks before interpreting narrow transmission features. The source uses a silicon device and a specific optomechanical readout. [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.121.040501) · [Author preprint, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00847)

COMSOL 6.4 provides a candidate independent 3D elastic comparison. Its Numeric Port workflow first computes a boundary mode at the chosen frequency, then computes complex scattering with one incident mode at a time. Begin with the uniform guide and the two-hole control at 1 GHz. Use the same free surfaces, rotated stiffness tensor and reference planes. Match modes by field overlap and check the signed physical flux independently. The documented ports represent propagating modes, so separately test sensitivity to evanescent fields at the ends. In a later frequency sweep, the frequency parameter must update both the Boundary Mode Analysis and Frequency Domain steps. [Elastic Port](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_solid.07.152.html) · [Mechanical multiport example](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.sme.mechanical_multiport_system/mechanical_multiport_system.html) · [Elastic tensor conventions](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_theory.06.026.html)

The 2026-11-16 h80 z4-to-z6 decision uses these methods as workflow guidance: compare the requested complex scattering observable, match modes across the changed section by field overlap, and update the compatible boundary modes when section discretization changes. The full device retains its original physical end planes and 200 nm thickness. The saved-field evanescent and polarization diagnostics select this control; they are not a proof that thickness error dominates. The QTCAD driven-Maxwell and COMSOL elastic-port pages were rechecked; their distinct electromagnetic and propagating-port formulations do not validate this full-discrete elastic radiation map.

The matched h60 uniform-control decision reuses the above separate uniform/defect-resolution strategy. QTCAD's driven-Maxwell tutorial and COMSOL's mechanical multiport example were rechecked on 2026-11-17 UTC: their useful lessons here are testing the complex scattering observable against a reference and keeping mode-wise power normalization explicit. The new control therefore compares both the h80/h60 complex response and a common finer SAFE reference, with original phases retained. Neither the electromagnetic example nor COMSOL's aluminum propagating-port model validates this anisotropic Ge device.

For the bounded section-frequency inventory, the COMSOL elastic Port page and the Gravenkamp–Plestenjak–Kiefer author abstract above were rechecked on 2026-11-17 UTC. COMSOL requires frequency-consistent boundary modes and inclusion of relevant propagating channels. The SAFE paper distinguishes crossings and avoided crossings from reliable branch tracing. The present inventory therefore recalculates modes for each new frequency and records finite signed channel counts and unresolved states, without treating sorted root indices as continuous physical branches. Its unchanged complete finite-section solver retains evanescent modes as well; this differs from COMSOL's documented propagating-port formulation and does not certify continuum completeness.

## Memory use and iterative linear solves

The opt-in batched assembler uses the same affine-element forms and quadrature with shared global DOF numbering, via the [scikit-fem CellBasis API](https://scikit-fem.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api.html#skfem.assembly.CellBasis). It releases evaluated cell fields after each batch; it does not reduce integration order or physical thickness resolution.

For the installed SciPy 1.18.1, the exact complex SuperLU factorization initially reserves four arrays at 30 times the input nonzero count. The [exact factorization source](https://github.com/scipy/scipy/blob/v1.18.1/scipy/sparse/linalg/_dsolve/SuperLU/SRC/zgstrf.c), [default fill estimate](https://github.com/scipy/scipy/blob/v1.18.1/scipy/sparse/linalg/_dsolve/SuperLU/SRC/sp_ienv.c) and [allocation code](https://github.com/scipy/scipy/blob/v1.18.1/scipy/sparse/linalg/_dsolve/SuperLU/SRC/zmemory.c) explain the measured pilot's private-commit peak; changing column ordering did not reduce that initial reservation. This is a version-specific implementation observation, not a lower bound on mathematical storage. Windows [private commit and working set](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/psapi/ns-psapi-process_memory_counters_ex) are reported separately.

The alternative uses [SciPy spilu](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.linalg.spilu.html) as a preconditioner for [GMRES](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.linalg.gmres.html). GMRES still solves the original complex elastic operator. Incomplete factorization is not accepted as the final answer: convergence status, residuals against the original matrix, and the existing scattering flux checks must pass. A useful preconditioner for one mesh or frequency need not converge for another; failed attempts remain evidence. Sparse-solver accuracy and spatial phase convergence remain separate checks.

[SciPy LGMRES](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.sparse.linalg.lgmres.html) retains augmentation vectors across restart cycles. A. H. Baker, E. R. Jessup and T. Manteuffel, *A Technique for Accelerating the Convergence of Restarted GMRES*, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications 26(4), 962-984 (2005), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479803422014), motivates this approach. Repeated startup slowdowns in the saved full-device residual history justify testing it here; scalar residual norms do not prove the paper's alternating-direction mechanism in this device.

SciPy 1.18.1 [checks LGMRES convergence before each outer update](https://github.com/scipy/scipy/blob/v1.18.1/scipy/sparse/linalg/_isolve/lgmres.py). A final update can meet tolerance while returning the exhausted iteration count. The new backend retains that raw status and, only when the returned vector already meets the requested original-equation tolerance and both residual gates, asks SciPy to check the same vector as its initial guess. Acceptance requires an immediate successful check with the vector unchanged. This confirms convergence without another factorization, a relaxed tolerance or discarded failure evidence.

E. Carson and N. J. Higham, *Accelerating the Solution of Linear Systems by Iterative Refinement in Three Precisions*, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 40(2), A817-A847 (2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1140819), motivates separating factorization, working and residual precision. Our optional complex64 ILU preconditioner with complex128 LGMRES equations is empirically checked against direct scattering and original residuals; it is not an implementation of that paper's three-precision refinement algorithm or a claim that its condition-number guarantees apply. The saved h160 continuation demonstrates lower factor storage permitting a higher fill setting on this machine, while retaining the unchanged scientific checks. It does not establish a universal speedup or forward-error bound.

## Saved-mode displacement-family diagnostics

COMSOL's [Eigenfrequency Studies: modal participation factors](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_theory.06.054.html) describes mass-weighted projections onto rigid translations and rotations, including the dependence of rotational participation on the reference point. Here that idea is adapted to complex SAFE displacement fields on one cross-section. It is not a COMSOL computation, an effective-mass result or a commercial-solver validation of this device.

With the saved consistent section mass M (kg/m) and a complex displacement column u, the Cartesian fraction is F_c = u_c† M_cc u_c / (u† M u). For a template b, the dimensionless score is |b† M u|² / [(b† M b)(u† M u)]. The templates are uniform laboratory x/y/z translations and axial rotation b_Rx = (0, -(z-zc), y-yc), centered at the section mass centroid. Template normalization removes their arbitrary units and amplitudes. At a common harmonic frequency the Cartesian fractions describe time-averaged kinetic-energy proportions; they are not transported-power or strain-energy fractions. Translation and rotation scores distinguish shapes that can have similar Cartesian proportions. A remaining orthogonal component is retained rather than forced into a family label. Scores and finite z4/z6 changes do not establish a causal scattering mechanism, continuous frequency branches or continuum uncertainty.

The independent verifier uses direct P2-field integration on each triangle. [NumPy's Gauss-Legendre documentation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.polynomial.legendre.leggauss.html) gives polynomial exactness through degree 2n-1 for n nodes. Under the Duffy map with barycentric coordinates (1-r, r(1-s), rs), a degree-four product of P2 fields times its Jacobian r has degree at most five in r and four in s, so a 3-by-3 tensor rule integrates it exactly up to floating-point arithmetic. This checks the saved-mass implementation without invoking its assembler or an eigensolver. Agreement is an implementation check, not a physical error estimate.

## Vector-mirror sector diagnostics

COMSOL 6.3 distinguishes [structural symmetry](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_solid.07.066.html), which constrains normal displacement at the plane, from [antisymmetry](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_solid.07.068.html), which constrains tangential displacement. Its [symmetry guidance](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.comsol/comsol_ref_modeling.19.040.html) requires compatible geometry, material, boundaries and sources. These references motivate checking the full polar-vector transformation; scalar component parity is insufficient. They are not a commercial-solver validation of this model.

For the accepted aligned cubic material, directly audit C_ijkl = D_ia D_jb D_kc D_ld C_abcd under Dy=diag(1,-1,1) and Dz=diag(1,1,-1). The intended centered geometry/free surfaces support those continuum mirrors. The implemented complex-linear actions retain x and k and use no complex conjugation. The commuting projectors P_ab=(I+a Ry)(I+b Rz)/4 yield dimensionless mass-content weights integral(rho |P_ab u|^2)/integral(rho |u|^2). Their sum is one, and rigid Tx/Ty/Tz/Rx fields lie in sectors (+,+)/(-,+)/(+,-)/(-,-). This projection argument is derived here; it is not an effective-mass or transmitted-power formula.

A mirrored rectangular P2 triangulation can change diagonals. For the validated grid, splitting each rectangular cell at its center gives a common refinement for both diagonal choices. Original and pulled-back fields remain quadratic on every piece, so degree-four triangle quadrature is exact up to floating-point arithmetic. Independent explicit P2/Duffy integration checks the implementation. Finite-sector mixtures, arbitrary bases near degeneracy and unproved full-device discrete symmetry limit interpretation. Sector content cannot be turned into a scattering-power leakage floor; shared sectors make coupling permissible, not causal or inevitable.
